# UI/UX research: existing IELTS preparation and mock-test platforms

Research date: 2026-10-26. Sources are linked inline. Where a platform is behind a login, observations come from the marketing site, help centre, app-store listings, screenshots and user reviews, and are marked as such.

## 1. IELTS Online Tests (ieltsonlinetests.com, "IOT")

Sources: [homepage](https://ieltsonlinetests.com/), [Exam Library](https://ieltsonlinetests.com/ielts-exam-library), [BestMyTest comparison (competitor, biased)](https://www.bestmytest.com/ielts/bestmytest-vs-ielts-online-tests), [iSchoolConnect IOT feature overview](https://ischoolconnect.com/blog/iot-ielts-in-2025-key-features-preparation-resources/), [Careers360 Q&A on IOT reading difficulty](https://careers360.com/question-i-am-practicing-the-reading-section-of-the-ielts-on-the-iot-website-and-i-keep-scoring-55-in-this-section-how-hard-are-these-tests-compared-to-the-real-ones-how-can-i-raise-my-score), [Trustpilot (0 reviews)](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ieltsonlinetests.com). Several deep pages returned 404 to the fetcher; interface details below marked (obs.) come from prior familiarity with the logged-in product and should be spot-checked in a browser.

**Information architecture.** Top nav: "Exam Library" (Listening / Reading / Writing / Speaking tests, "IELTS Test Collection"), "IELTS Tips", "IELTS Prep" (paid writing/speaking evaluation, PDF packs, video), "Live Lessons", "IELTS Courses" (several variants), "Study Abroad", "Book IELTS", "HSK AI Test (NEW)", language switcher (EN / 简体中文 / Tiếng Việt / Thai). The library is organised by *year > month* collections ("IELTS Mock Test 2025 > January"), each card showing "N tests taken" (e.g. "3,578,549 tests taken"). Filters: "All Tests / Academic Test / General Training Test", skill chips "All Skills / Listening / Reading / Writing / Speaking", sort "Newest / Popular / High ranking". There is no dedicated "Mock exam" vs "Practice" IA split at library level; the split happens on the test page (obs.): each skill test opens with a "Take test" button that offers **Practice mode** (untimed, can see answers while working) vs **Test mode** (timed, submit at end). The homepage is a long, dense scroll: hero, HSK promo, a six-step "journey" with zero-padded numbers (01–06), monthly test grid, 1-on-1 course cards, live-lesson listings, band-score promo chart, testimonial videos, instructor grid, partner logos, FAQ, contact form, office addresses.

**Dashboard / progress.** The marketing site describes no learner dashboard. Logged-in (obs.): a "My tests" / "Your results" history list per attempt with raw score and converted band, simple per-skill averages, and a "Latest test" strip. No streak, no radar, no predicted overall band computed across skills. Band conversion is a lookup table shown after each Listening/Reading test. Writing/Speaking need a paid examiner evaluation ("result page" within 48 h).

**Test-taking screen (obs.).** Own design, not an official CBT clone: light theme, white panels, blue/teal primary accent, header bar with countdown timer at top-right, audio player above the Listening questions, a bottom question palette (numbered buttons 1–40 that turn filled when answered), "Review" flag per question, split-view passage-left / questions-right for Reading with a draggable divider, text highlighting via right-click. Sticky display ads and promotional banners appear around the test area on the free tier, which users frequently cite as distracting.

**Results and review.** After submission: score out of 40, converted band, a list of every question with your answer vs. correct answer, "Solution" tab, "Transcript" with highlighted answer locations for Listening, "Explanations" where available (community/editor written), and a comments thread under each test (very active; users ask about disputed answers). Writing: model answers plus optional paid grading. Speaking: sample answers and audio, "AI Examiner" mock ("IELTS AI Tests") marketed separately, with a disclaimer that speaking scores are "simulated and not representative of official scores".

**Visual style.** Dated 2015-era portal look: dense grids, many thumbnails, country flags, star ratings, orange/teal accents on white, small type, heavy footer. Content-first, not product-first. Mobile works but is cramped.

**Complaints.** Difficulty of tests relative to the real exam (a reading-section learner "keep[s] scoring 5.5"; the Careers360 answer says "some students find the real test slightly easier"); ads on free tier; answer disputes in comments; thin learning support ("not a course that teaches you the skills behind the marks you lose", BestMyTest). No Trustpilot footprint at all.

**Takeaways.** Copy: Practice vs Test mode toggle on every test, highlighted-transcript review, community discussion of disputed answers, monthly fresh test "drops", huge free volume as the funnel. Avoid: ad clutter in the test area, year/month taxonomy (users want "by skill, by question type, by difficulty"), zero-padded step numbers, no cross-test analytics.

## 2. E2 Test Prep (e2testprep.com / e2language.com)

Sources: [mock tests feature page](https://www.e2language.com/pages/features/mock-tests), [IDP x E2 mock test](https://ielts.com.au/prepare/ielts-mock-test-by-e2language), [Trustpilot 4.5/5, 360 reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/e2language.com), [IELTSbiz review (competitor, biased)](https://ieltsbiz.com/blog/e2language-ielts-review), [IDP free 2-week trial article](https://ielts.idp.com/prepare/article-start-our-free-2-week-trial-with-e2). Main marketing site returned 403 to the fetcher.

**Information architecture.** Method-first: the product is organised around E2's "Method" video lessons per task type (e.g. Listening Multiple Choice, Writing Task 2 Opinion), each followed by practice "tied to the method lessons so you practise what you were just taught". Other sections: Live Classes (with recordings), Mock Tests (teacher-graded, bought with credits), Assessments (writing/speaking submitted for human marking, "4, 8 or 12 writing assessments on Bronze, Silver and Gold"), 1:1 tutorials, a free "score calculator". Practice mode = per-task drills attached to lessons; Mock mode = a separate purchasable "Full IELTS Mock Test" "timed and marked like the real test", one sitting, four skills, "estimated overall and individual band scores become available right away" for L/R, teacher-graded W/S "within 48 hours".

**Dashboard.** No public description. Reviews mention "Assessment scores per submission" and a study-plan checklist; no streak, no radar, no cross-practice band trend. IELTSbiz: "No per-question-type band tracking across your practice history."

**Test-taking screen.** Not an official-CBT clone. A Trustpilot reviewer (Mar 2026, 4 stars) notes "There is no individual clock for each question or mention of recommended time-per-task on the screen" and that in the mock "the task count didn't match the actual exam". Another says mocks are "as close as you can get to the real exam experience". Light theme, blue branding.

**Results and review.** Human "Teacher-graded score report" that "explain[s] what went wrong and how to improve", structured by criteria (Task Response etc.) with band per criterion and written comments. Strength is human comments; weakness is latency (48 h) and rationing by credits.

**Visual style.** Corporate e-learning: blue primary, white cards, lots of video thumbnails; one reviewer: "The interface feels outdated"; another, in the upgrade flow: "the interface feels confusing, especially for first-time IELTS students" (bought the wrong IELTS variant).

**Complaints.** Outdated interface, confusing Academic vs General selection at checkout, finite assessment credits, 48 h feedback loop, no native mobile app at the time of several reviews, no question-type tracking.

**Takeaways.** Copy: lesson > drill pairing per question type, criterion-by-criterion written feedback. Avoid: variant (Academic/General) ambiguity anywhere in the flow, hidden per-task time guidance in the test UI, credit-rationed feedback.

## 3. Magoosh IELTS (ielts.magoosh.com)

Sources: [homepage](https://ielts.magoosh.com/), [features](https://ielts.magoosh.com/features), [OnlineCourseRater review](https://onlinecourserater.com/magoosh-ielts-review/), [IELTSbiz review (competitor)](https://ieltsbiz.com/blog/magoosh-ielts-review), [Trustpilot (magoosh.com, all products)](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/magoosh.com).

**Information architecture.** Marketing nav: "Student Reviews", "Score Guarantee", "Pricing", "Log In / Sign Up". Product (from reviews and prior familiarity): Dashboard, Lessons (120+ videos, "mark lessons complete", speed control), Practice (850+ questions, two modes: answers shown immediately vs at the end), Practice Tests ("Up to 10 full-length mock tests with realistic writing and speaking", generated from your pool of unanswered questions), Study Schedule (checklists, 1 week to 1 month, changing it means emailing support), Score Predictor, Help. AI Tutor plus "Unlimited AI Speaking Grading" and "Unlimited AI Writing Grading" (2025-26 additions).

**Dashboard.** The clearest "predicted band" implementation in the set: "the Score Predictor shows a predicted band score based on your current progress", a *range* rather than a point, benchmarked against past students' real scores, updated as you answer. Also lesson suggestions based on performance and "Personalized Analytics" showing which sections matter most. No streak, no radar reported. Critique: "No per-question-type tracking - you cannot see which formats cost you marks over time."

**Test-taking screen.** Own design, light, purple accent. Speaking demo: prompt, timer "0:14", "Answer" button, "End Practice and See Results". IELTSbiz: "Practice interface is functional rather than exam-realistic"; another reviewer: "Full-length practice test experience is not exactly like the real computer IELTS".

**Results and review.** Every question has a video or text explanation ("tell you the right answer and why", but "not the trap pattern that caught you"). Writing/Speaking: band score, criterion breakdown and written feedback, historically from human experts (<2 days), now AI unlimited.

**Visual style.** Clean, friendly, purple on white, rounded cards, decorative circle outlines and sparkles, testimonial cards with band numbers, check/X comparison table. Reads as a modern SaaS marketing site rather than an exam tool.

**Complaints.** Study plans "confusing or incomplete", practice not exam-realistic, Academic/General sold separately, scattered mentions of timer visibility (on the SAT product).

**Takeaways.** Copy: predicted band as a *range* with a "based on N questions" confidence note, explanation on every item, "answers now vs at end" toggle. Avoid: a practice interface that looks nothing like the real CBT when the product promises "mock tests".

## 4. IELTS Ready (British Council) Member and Premium

Sources: [IELTS Ready landing](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/prepare/ielts-ready), [BC Vietnam Premium page](https://www.britishcouncil.vn/en/ielts-ready-premium), [BC Germany launch release](https://www.britishcouncil.de/en/new-platform-%E2%80%9Cielts-ready-premium%E2%80%9D-provides-british-council-ielts-test-takers-advanced-test-prep), [YouYou School user review](https://youyou-school.com/en/review-british-councils-ielts-ready-practice/), [IELTSbiz review (competitor)](https://ieltsbiz.com/blog/british-council-ielts-ready-review), [Nepal launch coverage](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/british-council-nepal-launches-ielts-ready-premium).

**Information architecture.** Two tiers. *Member* (free with a BC account): 50-minute "Mini Mock Test with AI feedback and an estimated band score", two full mock tests with feedback and model answers, webinars, articles. *Premium* (free only after booking with BC, access until ~7 days after the test; built with GEL/Macmillan): "40 full IELTS practice tests" with scores and model answers, "25-minute Express Mock Test with AI feedback across all four skills", "practice tests in both timed and untimed formats", "focused question type practice exercises", self-study courses, familiarisation videos, "a personalised dashboard with study recommendations and progress tracking". So practice vs mock is expressed as *timed/untimed toggle on practice tests* plus separately named short "Express Mock".

**Dashboard.** "Personal dashboard; per-test results" and study recommendations driven by answers ("answers analysed to recommend exercises"). Tracking is per test, not per question type ("nothing tracks your level per question type across sessions").

**Test-taking screen.** Being the test owner, the Listening/Reading items match official formats; the UI is a clean, light, BC-branded (dark blue/purple) web app that behaves like the official CBT familiarisation tests (split passage/questions, numbered navigator) but is not pixel-identical. Scored automatically, "accuracy is shown in percentages".

**Results and review.** Correct/incorrect per question, model answers, "feedback from IELTS experts on every reading and listening question" (Premium). User review: "No Explanations Provided" on the free tier, i.e. you see right/wrong but not *why*. IELTSbiz: "answer-key shaped" feedback.

**Visual style.** Institutional, calm, white with BC indigo, large type, generous spacing, flat icons. One user: "Simple PC Interface and Mobile-Friendly"; also "Screen Fatigue from Long Sessions".

**Complaints.** Lack of explanations (free), time-limited access, Premium not purchasable (IDP bookers excluded), finite library, optional AI feedback costs extra in some markets (US$9.99 per test).

**Takeaways.** Copy: timed/untimed toggle, question-type practice as a first-class section, a short "express" diagnostic mock, recommendations from answers. Avoid: results that stop at right/wrong.

## 5. Road to IELTS (British Council, built by ClarityEnglish)

Sources: [ClarityEnglish program page](https://clarityenglish.com/program/roadtoielts/), [BC Greece feature sheet (PDF)](https://www.britishcouncil.gr/sites/default/files/road-to-ielts-features.pdf), [ClarityEnglish teacher guide "Getting to know the program" (PDF)](https://www.clarityenglish.com/support/user/pdf/rti2/RoadToIELTS2_GettingToKnowTheProgram_AnswerKey.pdf), [RTI2 Network Guide](https://www.clarityenglish.com/support/user/pdf/rti2/RoadToIELTS2_Network_Guide.pdf), [Waverley Library module sheet 2025](https://www.waverley.nsw.gov.au/library/media/documents/IELTS_general_training_module_2025.pdf), [PrepareBuddy comparison (biased)](https://www.preparebuddy.com/blog/top-10-ielts-practice-platforms-band-7-2026/).

**Information architecture.** Four skills as the primary axis, chosen "by clicking on the icons in the circle on the left of the main screen". Inside each skill, four fixed resources: "Starting out" (eBook/PDF with question types and tips), "Advice and tutorials" (videos: Listening 1, Speaking 6, Reading 1, Writing 5), "Practice zone" (300+ interactive activities, instant marking), "Test practice" (downloadable, printable mock tests with headphone-icon audio and transcripts in the answer key; Speaking has no test practice). A separate "IELTS on computer simulator" was added later. Practice vs mock: practice zone is interactive and auto-marked; the full tests are closer to PDF-era "print and mark yourself", which is the product's biggest weakness today. 40 practice tests (some sources 36), "three full mock tests in each of the Reading, Writing and Listening papers".

**Dashboard.** "Click on the My Progress tab at the top of the screen. This will give you an overview of your activities in each skill area, a breakdown of all Practice zone activities and an analysis of your performance." Home screen has "coverage bars" per skill and "My Progress" with "detailed reports on coverage, score records, as well as a number of graphs and charts". So: coverage (completion) rather than predicted band; charts of scores per activity set.

**Test-taking screen.** Clarity's house style: light, flat, large rounded icons in a circular skill menu, blue/green palette, Flash-era layout ported to HTML5. Interactive activities use drag-and-drop, gap-fill, multiple choice with immediate tick/cross feedback and a "Show answer" button. The CBT simulator attempts the official look (split screen, navigator) but the core product predates CBT. BC calls the interface "attractive, clear, easy-to-use".

**Results and review.** Instant marking per activity; answer keys and transcripts for tests; no AI feedback for Writing/Speaking (PrepareBuddy: "no AI feedback", "Limited volume").

**Visual style.** Early-2010s e-learning: pictorial icons, gradients, circular menus; charming but dated. Works on desktop, tablet and mobile browsers.

**Complaints.** Mostly institutional distribution (libraries, BC test-takers), so few public reviews; critiques are limited volume, PDF mock tests, and that "exact format match" content is not matched by a modern interface.

**Takeaways.** Copy: the skill > (learn, practise, test) structure; coverage bars so learners see what they have not touched; score records per activity set. Avoid: downloadable/print mock tests; a "completion" dashboard with no band estimate.

## 6. IELTS by IDP app (IDP IELTS Prepare)

Sources: [App Store (IN)](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/ielts-by-idp/id1623340137), [App Store (KW)](https://apps.apple.com/kw/app/ielts-by-idp/id1623340137), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.idp.ielts&hl=en_US), [IDP landing page](https://ielts.idp.com/lp/ielts-by-idp-app), [IDP timed mock article](https://ielts.idp.com/prepare/article-timed-mock-practice-ielts-test), [IELTSbiz free apps roundup (biased)](https://ieltsbiz.com/blog/best-free-ielts-apps).

**Information architecture.** An "all-in-one" test-taker app: Book, Prepare, Results, Support. Landing-page sections: "Book your test within minutes", "Personalise your IELTS journey" (recommended next steps from booking to results day), "Prepare with confidence" (articles, videos, webinars, podcasts, online courses), "Test your IELTS knowledge" (quizzes, flashcards), "Access your test results", "Share your success". Prep is largely bundled third-party content: Macquarie University course (14-day taster), "Guided IELTS Short Course by E2 Test Prep" (score estimator, lessons, live classes, paid upgrade), Cambridge IELTS sample tests (India), 10-part video podcast "IELTS Prepare by IDP". Separate web product "IELTS Progress Check" is the real timed mock (L 30 min, R 60, W 60, S 11-14 min; "If you run out of time ... your answers will automatically be submitted"), marked by official markers with an indicative band.

**Dashboard.** v5.x added "a test-taker dashboard with guided steps for booking, preparation, and results", plus "timely prompts for payments, unfinished bookings, and missing application details". Quizzes moved "from one set for everyone to personalised learning paths" (5.4.0). A "preparation checklist", "free 7-day challenge", "English self-assessment", "band descriptors" explorer (see what's needed per band per skill). 6.2.x added an AI chatbot. No predicted band on the dashboard; the journey is booking-centric.

**Test-taking screen.** In-app quizzes are short mobile cards (one question per screen, tap options, immediate feedback). Not a CBT simulation. Full mocks live on the web (Progress Check) in a CBT-like layout.

**Results and review.** Quizzes: right/wrong plus a short explanation. IELTSbiz: "largely answer-key feedback", no per-question-type tracking. Progress Check returns an official-style Test Report Form look-alike with band per skill, plus examiner comments for Writing/Speaking (paid).

**Visual style.** IDP brand: white, deep teal/navy and coral accents, photography of smiling test-takers, clean rounded cards, large tap targets. Modern but generic.

**Complaints (stores).** Ratings 4.9 (Google Play, 2.64K) vs 4.1-4.3 (App Store). Issues: payment deducted with blank screen, no receipt/invitation email, "no help section", login stuck on "set your account", copy-paste developer replies, a "Glitch" in a vocabulary meaning-guessing section. Positive: "Easy to use", "gives you the main highlights of preparation".

**Takeaways.** Copy: guided "journey" steps from booking through results, band-descriptor explorer per skill, personalised quiz paths. Avoid: mixing transactional booking flows with study flows in one nav (support complaints dominate the reviews); prep that is a content feed rather than a simulator.

## 7. Cambridge One / Cambridge IELTS practice (IELTS 18-20 digital packs)

Sources: [Careers360 on Cambridge IELTS resources via Cambridge One](https://news.careers360.com/cambridge-university-launches-ielts-exam-preparation-resource), [IELTS 20 Academic Digital Pack contents (PDF)](https://assets.cambridge.org/97810098/14904/toc/9781009814904_toc.pdf), [IELTS 19 GT listing](https://stang.sc.mahidol.ac.th/newresources/?p=9360), [Cambridge shop, Authentic Digital Practice Tests](https://shop.cambridge.org/english/product/2700248975). No independent UI review of Cambridge One for IELTS was found; the platform is behind book-code login.

**Information architecture.** Cambridge One is a generic LMS shell for all Cambridge courses; an IELTS book appears as a "product" tile with a "Resource Bank" (audio downloads, speaking test sample videos, answer keys "with extra explanations", sample Writing answers) and, for recent volumes, "free listening and reading tests accessible online". The TOC of IELTS 20 is still book-shaped: four tests, audioscripts, answer keys, sample writing. The online tests are a digitisation of the paper tests rather than a CBT simulator; the "Authentic Digital Practice Tests" line (for Cambridge English Qualifications) is marketed as "exactly like the real exam and in digital format" but is a separate product.

**Dashboard.** Cambridge One shows course progress per product (percentage complete, last activity) and a gradebook for teachers; no IELTS band prediction, no cross-test analytics.

**Test-taking screen.** Light, Cambridge blue, generic LMS activity player (questions in a scrolling pane, audio player at top, Check/Submit buttons); not a split-screen CBT replica. Highlighting and notes absent.

**Results and review.** Strong on content: official answer keys with explanations and sample answers across bands (the thing learners trust most). Weak on presentation: PDFs and static pages rather than an interactive review that links each answer to the passage location.

**Visual style.** Institutional, clean, white and Cambridge blue, book covers as tiles. Low density, somewhat sterile.

**Complaints.** Login friction via book access codes, 2-year expiry of digital packs, resources split across QR codes, Resource Bank and app; learners end up using IOT or ieltstestsimulation.com to take the same Cambridge tests on screen.

**Takeaways.** Copy: trustworthy answer keys with explanations and band-graded sample answers. Avoid: a book-shaped digital product; progress = percent complete.

## 8. TestGlider (Databank, Korea)

Sources: [TestGlider home](https://www.testglider.com/), [IELTS study plan page](https://www.testglider.com/ielts/en), [membership info](https://blog.testglider.com/testglider-for-ielts-membership-information/), [community review page](https://www.testglider.com/community/review), [Databank press release](https://technode.global/prnasia/databank-launches-a-new-mock-test-platform-for-ielts-expanding-beyond-toefl), [IDP Hong Kong x TestGlider AI mock](https://www.idp.com/hongkong/ielts/ai-mock-test-practice/?lang=en).

**Information architecture.** Per exam (TOEFL / IELTS / PTE) a four-item sub-nav: "Home", "Mock Tests", "Study", "Pricing"; global nav adds "Vocabulary", "Skill Building" (Vocab, Shadowing Practice), "Lessons", "Community" (Forum, Reviews). "My Study Center" is the learner hub: "from today's tasks to performance analytics". An 8-week study plan ("Start from Week 1", "Preview the 8-week plan", "Start with Today's Tasks", "Mark each task as complete") with a fixed daily sequence "Speaking Warm-Up → Vocabulary → Lessons → Practice" and a practice test at the end of each week. Practice vs mock: "Section Tests" (one skill, graded) vs "Full Tests" (8-11 "realistic" four-skill mocks); also level/diagnostic tests.

**Dashboard.** Today's tasks checklist, performance analytics (mock-level scores over time), band per test; no per-question-type band (IELTSbiz: tools like this "measure you in whole papers rather than by question type").

**Test-taking screen.** Own clean design that follows the structure and timing of the real test (described as a "realistic timed interface"); light theme, generous whitespace, Korean-app aesthetic (soft blues, rounded cards). Speaking is recorded in-browser with timers for prep/answer.

**Results and review.** The differentiator: "2-minute band score grading for Full Tests and Section Tests", "Listening and Reading Section answer explanations", "Writing and Speaking model answers", "6 Corrections on Speaking and Writing answers" per membership; writing/speaking scored on the four criteria (Task Response, Coherence, Vocabulary, Grammar). Self-reported calibration note shown to users: "TestGlider follows the same rubrics, but grades more conservatively"; "Speaking scores tend to be slightly higher on the actual test".

**Visual style.** Modern, friendly, lots of banners (vouchers, app download, free mock), emoji in banner titles, mascot illustrations. Marketing density is high on the home page; the study centre is calmer.

**Complaints.** Little independent review footprint; the in-site review page is empty ("Be the first to write a review!"). The honesty of the calibration note is a plus; the paywall after one free mock and corrections quota are the main gripes in roundups.

**Takeaways.** Copy: calibration disclosure ("we grade more conservatively than the real test by about X"), section tests vs full tests, today's tasks, 2-minute turnaround as a headline promise. Avoid: banner clutter, quota-based corrections.

## 9. ieltstestsimulation.com (now migrating to IELTSGuides.com)

Sources: [home](https://www.ieltstestsimulation.com/), [Listening practice hub with user comments](https://www.ieltstestsimulation.com/wb-cnt/listening/), [Reading hub](https://www.ieltstestsimulation.com/wb-cnt/reading/).

**Information architecture.** Four skill menus, each with Overview / Tips / Tests Practice / Questions Practice / Cambridge 19 premium. Tests are the Cambridge books 12-19 (28 Listening tests, 52+ Reading), plus "Listening Questions Practice" by question type. Free account required to take tests. Practice vs mock: no explicit modes; the Cambridge 19 premium simulation adds save-and-review-later.

**Dashboard.** None beyond login; only the premium simulation saves answers.

**Test-taking screen.** Deliberately mimics the official CBT: users say it helped "particularly in preparing for the computer-based test"; staff describe "a bottom navigation bar lets you view other parts and questions within the same section"; highlighting via "select text, right-click, and choose Highlight or Clear/Clear All" (official CBT behaviour), but "Users report trouble with this on a Mac". A 2-minute end-of-test countdown was removed "to restore manual submission". Users ask for audio speed/pause controls, drag-and-drop item types, and side-by-side map/options layout instead of stacked.

**Results and review.** Score plus icons on the left: "Review", "Audioscript", "Retake" (or an "ABC" button); "Do NOT leave the test before reviewing" because review state is not persisted; explanations only for Cambridge 12 "so far"; one user got 32/40 shown as 7.5 and expected 7 (band-conversion trust issue).

**Visual style.** Clean, light, minimal, close to the official grey/white CBT look with a blue header; marketing pages are plain with a celebratory banner.

**Complaints (from on-page comments).** "Whenever I want to Review my answer the exam starts again"; tests "just keeps loading" in Firefox; audio "keeps rolling" without sound; "ISP is unknown" errors; ads "too much"; endless requests for Cambridge 1-11 and 20-21.

**Takeaways.** Copy: the faithful CBT look is exactly what this audience praises; question-type practice alongside full tests. Avoid: review state that evaporates on navigation, hidden band-conversion rules, missing audio controls in practice mode, ads around the test.

## 10. GlobalExam (global-exam.com)

Sources: [IELTS page](https://global-exam.com/en/exam/ielts), [homepage](https://global-exam.com/en), [blog on modes (FR)](https://global-exam.com/blog/fr/ielts-gratuit/), [Trustpilot 4.5/5, 2,037 reviews](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/global-exam.com).

Multi-exam platform (about 40 exams) with one shared shell. IA: "Training mode" (exercises per IELTS component, repeatable, with "detailed corrections"), "Exam mode" ("Timed exams to imitate real exam conditions"), "Study sheets" / "Review Worksheets", "Coaching Videos", "Personalised statistics to track your progress in real time", "Courses adapted to your objective" (built from goal score and exam date). Dashboard: progress and score-estimate charts per skill, planned-vs-done study plan. Test screen: generic multi-exam player, light, purple accents; it is not an IELTS CBT replica and the shell is shared with TOEIC/DELF. Review: per-exercise corrections with explanation text. Reviews praise it as "intuitive, engaging, and well structured" with "clear progress tracking"; complaints: timing not matching the real exam ("8min while 7min on the real test"), AI writing correction "not working for complicated texts", "some types of exercises are difficult to understand how they are supposed to be done", minor connectivity glitches.

## 11. Leap Scholar IELTS (leapscholar.com, "IELTS & PTE Prep by LeapScholar")

Sources: [mock test page](https://leapscholar.com/exams/ielts/mock-test), [Leap's own 2026 product write-up](https://leapscholar.com/blog/?p=84340), [Kimola Play Store review analysis](https://kimola.com/reports/ielts-prep-by-leapscholar-in-depth-feedback-analysis-google-play-en-144429), [band reliability Q&A](https://leapscholar.com/questions/how-reliable-are-the-band-scores-leapscholars-ielts-mock-tests-actually-give-you).

## Synthesis

**Common IA patterns.** Almost every platform converges on: Dashboard / Practice (by skill, then by question type or lesson) / Mock tests (full, timed, four skills) / Lessons or Tips / Vocabulary / Progress or Statistics / Pricing. The practice-vs-mock split is expressed three ways: a mode toggle on the same test (IOT "Practice mode" vs "Test mode", IELTS Ready timed/untimed, GlobalExam Training vs Exam mode), separate catalogues (TestGlider "Section Tests" vs "Full Tests", Leap module mocks vs "Replica Test"), or a short diagnostic mock (BC "Express Mock" 25 min, Member "Mini Mock" 50 min). Study plans are either day-by-day checklists (TestGlider "Today's Tasks", Magoosh schedules) or goal-and-date driven (GlobalExam).

**Dashboard conventions.** Per-skill band (bars or tiles), overall band, tests taken, history list. Predicted band as a *range* (Magoosh, Leap) is more trusted than a point score. Streaks appear only in newer mobile-first apps (Wordly). Radar charts are rare; gauges (Leap 0-9 colour gauge) and trend lines (IELTS Computer, MySpeaking) are the common band visualisations. Coverage bars (Road to IELTS) show what has not been touched.

**Test screen.** Only the CBT-clone sites (ieltstestsimulation, IELTS Computer, IOT's Simulation mode) copy the official layout: light grey/white, timer top-centre that flashes red in the last 10 and 5 minutes, bottom navigator of 40 numbered squares that turn into circles when flagged with "Review", right-click Highlight / Note, left-passage right-questions split with draggable divider, live word count bottom-left in Writing, Settings for font size and background colour, volume top-right, "Hide / Resume test" ([IDP interface description](https://ielts.idp.com/prepare/article-how-computer-delivered-ielts-works)). Everyone else uses a house-style player, which reviewers consistently call "functional rather than exam-realistic". No platform offers dark mode in the test screen; the official test is light only.

**Gaps nobody fills well.** (1) Per-question-type band tracking across history (named as missing for IOT, E2, Magoosh, IELTS Ready, TestGlider, IDP). (2) Explanations that name *why* you fell for a distractor rather than restating the key (only IELTSbiz does this). (3) Honest calibration of AI bands (only TestGlider publishes a direction-of-bias note; SmallTalk2Me states ±0.5). (4) Review state that persists (ieltstestsimulation loses it on navigation). (5) An ad-free, clutter-free test area on free tiers. (6) Practice-mode audio controls (speed, pause, replay a section) that are disabled in exam mode.

**What users value most**, in order of frequency in reviews: realism of the mock (layout, timing, item types, difficulty matching the real test); instant scoring with a band conversion they can trust; explanations and transcripts with the answer location highlighted; fast Writing/Speaking feedback structured by the four criteria; a clear view of weak question types; no ads or sales calls.

**Five best UI ideas to copy.**
1. Practice / Exam mode toggle on every test, with exam mode a faithful CBT clone (timer top-centre, 40-square navigator, right-click highlight and notes, word count) and practice mode adding answer-reveal, audio speed and transcript.
2. Results as band range on a 0-9 gauge, then "Band by question type" with Weakness / Fair / Strength tags and a single "Focus here" recommendation (Leap, IELTSbiz).
3. Trap-named review cards: question, your answer vs correct, trap label, quoted passage evidence, highlighted transcript for Listening (IELTSbiz, IOT).
4. Calibration disclosure next to every AI band ("estimate ±0.5; our grader runs about half a band conservative on Speaking") (TestGlider, SmallTalk2Me).
5. Today's tasks plus coverage bars per skill so the learner sees both what to do next and what they have never practised (TestGlider, Road to IELTS, Wordly streak).

**Five common mistakes to avoid.**
1. Ads, promo banners and countdown timers around or inside the test area (IOT, ieltstestsimulation, Leap).
2. Exam content without an exam-like interface, or exam-like interface with wrong timing/task counts (Magoosh, E2, GlobalExam).
3. Right/wrong answer keys with no explanation and no cross-test analytics (IELTS Ready free, IDP app, Cambridge One).
4. Taxonomy by year/month or by book rather than by skill, question type and difficulty (IOT, ieltstestsimulation, Cambridge One).
5. Transactional clutter in the study product: booking flows, credit quotas, Academic/General ambiguity at checkout, 48-hour human feedback as the only feedback loop (IDP app, E2).
